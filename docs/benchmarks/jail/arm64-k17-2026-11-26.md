# ARM64 K17 on the recorded executable, 26 November 2026

**K17 passed five replays in a row on the executable of the recorded ARM64 conformance run.** Every file-operation overhead stayed under the 100% ceiling. The narrowest margin was 1.57 points. With this record the ARM64 gates are confirmed, and stable releases from 0.1.1 ship an ARM64 archive. [K17 record](results/linux-arm64-k17-2026-11-26/k17.json) · [Conformance run](arm64-stable-qualification-2026-11-23.md)

## What was measured

The executable is the one the 23 November conformance run tested: SHA-256 `b01c07d5a378bce363d1df11e5b5ed501cdabd38ab013ade0cd6452aec1d66ad`, built at `433e794bc09a9f774d7d421bf7d0b94482f2a9a2`, with build-input digest `sha256:0da6ea53bcb2f25c2094adf95ddc5e13ace97c688222b881d17e8cbbd1404c92`. Its hash was checked before and after the replays. The guest ran Ubuntu 26.04.1 LTS, kernel `7.0.0-43-generic`, glibc 2.43-2ubuntu2.7 and bubblewrap 0.11.1-1ubuntu0.3 on 4 native ARM64 vCPUs of an Apple M5 Pro under QEMU HVF.

Each replay used the seed and settings of every earlier ARM64 replay: 30 measured launches per arm in 18 cells, one warmup per arm and a guest load limit of 3. The criterion was declared before the replays ran: five in a row, every one passing. It followed the seven replays of 23 November, which alternated between passing and failing on the same image and executable. [23 November record](arm64-stable-qualification-2026-11-23.md)

## How narrow the pass is

| Session | Highest overhead | Margin under the ceiling |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Plain | 98.43% | 1.57 points |
| Delegated | 97.16% | 2.84 points |

The margin is narrow. The overhead was between 95.59% and 98.43% in the plain session and between 94.15% and 97.16% in the delegated one. Every startup result was far below the 500 ms limit; the worst was 51.37 ms.

The host was not idle by a strict standard. Its one-minute load was 3.9 to 5.3 on 18 cores during the 40 samples, with a third-party process above 40% CPU in 4 samples and pages swapped in during 2. Nothing was compiled. The replays passed under that load.

## What is not claimed

K17 is one configuration's measurement, an HVF virtual machine on this Mac. The Raspberry Pi and OrbStack results remain separate records. A host under heavier load can exceed the ceiling, as three of the seven replays of 23 November did.
